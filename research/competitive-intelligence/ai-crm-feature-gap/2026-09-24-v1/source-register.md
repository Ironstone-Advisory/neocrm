# First-party source register

**Research cutoff:** 2026-09-24  
**Evidence policy:** Prefer current official product, help, pricing, release-note, developer, regulator, and government sources. Vendor pages establish represented capability and packaging; they do not independently prove performance, ROI, accuracy, safety, or customer outcomes.

## Status and confidence

- **GA/current** — presented as currently available without a beta/preview limitation.
- **Rolling out** — released progressively.
- **Beta / preview / pilot / early access / announced** — retained at full capability-value score; status is recorded separately.
- **Unclear-current** — marketed now, but the official page does not disclose a precise lifecycle or entitlement.
- **High confidence** — explicit first-party product/help/pricing evidence.
- **Medium confidence** — first-party positioning exists, but entitlement, detailed behavior, or lifecycle is incomplete.

All sources below were reviewed for this dated proposal. Prices can change and should be rechecked before a purchase, publication, or formal pricing decision.

The inventory uses a controlled `market_status` vocabulary: `GA/current`, `Mixed: GA/current + rolling out`, `Mixed: GA/current + preview`, `Mixed: GA/current + beta`, `Mixed: GA/current + pilot`, and `Mixed: GA/current + announced`. A mixed value describes the maturity range across the representative products in that row; it does not imply that every named product has the same status.

## Evidence limitations

- Feature existence is not proof of usefulness, accuracy, compliance, or safe autonomy.
- Vendor terminology and packaging can change faster than architecture documents.
- Public pages frequently omit negotiated minimums, implementation cost, capacity rules, regional restrictions, and complete lifecycle status.
- Custom-price products cannot be compared reliably on total cost without quotes and a normalized workload.
- Release notes may describe capabilities scheduled later than the research cutoff; those are explicitly tagged preview, early access, rolling out, or announced.
- The capability matrix records product strength based on official descriptions, not hands-on product testing.
- Canadian rules must be applied to a concrete organization, jurisdiction, data practice, and message purpose; this research is product guidance, not legal advice.
